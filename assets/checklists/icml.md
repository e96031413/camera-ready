# ICML Paper Checklist — Question Set

Source: ICML author instructions, <https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorInstructions>.
Answer options: Yes / No / NA.

## 1. Claims
**Question:** Do the abstract and introduction state claims that match the theoretical and experimental evidence actually presented?
**Guidance:** Every claim needs a section that supports it. Trim claims the experiments do not reach.

## 2. Limitations
**Question:** Does the paper discuss the limitations of the work, including the settings where the method is expected to fail?
**Guidance:** A dedicated Limitations section is the safest way to satisfy this.

## 3. Theoretical Assumptions and Proofs
**Question:** For each theoretical result, does the paper state the full set of assumptions and give a complete proof?
**Guidance:** Proof sketches in the main text are acceptable if the full proof is in the appendix. NA if the paper has no theoretical results.

## 4. Reproducibility of Experimental Results
**Question:** Does the paper disclose everything needed to reproduce the main experimental results?
**Guidance:** Data, preprocessing, model, training procedure, evaluation protocol, and code availability.

## 5. Open Access to Code and Data
**Question:** Does the paper provide access to code and data, or explain why it cannot?
**Guidance:** An anonymized repository link satisfies this at submission time. A stated licensing or privacy restriction is a valid No with justification.

## 6. Experimental Settings and Details
**Question:** Does the paper specify data splits, hyperparameters, how hyperparameters were selected, and the optimizer used?
**Guidance:** Hyperparameter search protocol matters as much as the final values.

## 7. Statistical Significance
**Question:** Does the paper report error bars, confidence intervals, or another measure of variability for the main results?
**Guidance:** Multiple seeds with standard error is the usual bar. A single run without variance reporting should answer No with justification.

## 8. Compute Resources
**Question:** Does the paper report the compute resources — hardware type, count, memory, and total runtime — needed to reproduce the experiments?
**Guidance:** Include the cost of failed and preliminary runs, not just the final reported ones.

## 9. Code of Ethics
**Question:** Does the research conform to the ICML Code of Ethics?
**Guidance:** Cannot be inferred automatically — the author must confirm this.

## 10. Broader Impact
**Question:** Does the paper discuss the potential positive and negative societal consequences of the work?
**Guidance:** ICML requires a Broader Impact Statement. NA is rarely appropriate; if the work is purely foundational, say so explicitly rather than skipping the statement.

## 11. Safeguards
**Question:** For released models, data, or code with a risk of misuse, does the paper describe the safeguards applied?
**Guidance:** NA if nothing high-risk is released.

## 12. Licenses and Credit for Existing Assets
**Question:** Are code, data, and models produced by others correctly cited, with their licenses and terms of use stated and respected?
**Guidance:** Cannot be inferred automatically — the author must confirm the licenses were checked.

## 13. New Assets
**Question:** Are new assets introduced by the paper documented, with the documentation released alongside them?
**Guidance:** NA if the paper releases no new assets.

## 14. Human Subjects and Crowdsourcing
**Question:** For studies with human participants or crowdworkers, does the paper give the full instructions, the compensation, and the recruitment procedure?
**Guidance:** NA if the paper involves no human subjects.

## 15. Institutional Review
**Question:** For research with human subjects, does the paper state whether IRB approval or an equivalent review was obtained?
**Guidance:** NA if the paper involves no human subjects research.
