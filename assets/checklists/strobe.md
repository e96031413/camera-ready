# STROBE — Question Set (observational studies)

Source: STROBE statement, https://www.strobe-statement.org/ (checked 2026-09-05).
Answer options: Yes / No / NA. Every answer needs a location or a reason.

This file paraphrases the STROBE items. The guideline numbers 22 items; item 1
has two lettered parts, listed here as separate rows, giving 23 rows in total. Items 6, 12, 14, 15 and 17 have
design-specific parts; answer the part matching your design and mark the
others NA.

Use this for cohort, case-control and cross-sectional studies.

## 1a. Title and abstract
**Question:** Does the title or abstract name the study design with a commonly used term?
**Guidance:** "Cohort study", "case-control study", "cross-sectional study".

## 1b. Abstract summary
**Question:** Does the abstract give an informative and balanced summary of what was done and found?
**Guidance:** Include the effect estimate, not just the direction.

## 2. Background
**Question:** Is the scientific background and rationale explained?
**Guidance:** Say what the study adds to existing observational evidence.

## 3. Objectives
**Question:** Are specific objectives, including any prespecified hypotheses, stated?
**Guidance:** Exploratory aims should be labelled as such.

## 4. Study design
**Question:** Are key elements of the study design presented early in the paper?
**Guidance:** One paragraph a reader can classify the study from.

## 5. Setting
**Question:** Are setting, locations, and relevant dates given, including recruitment, exposure, follow-up and data collection periods?
**Guidance:** Dates, not durations alone.

## 6. Participants
**Question:** Are eligibility criteria, sources and selection methods given for the design used?
**Guidance:** Cohort: how follow-up worked. Case-control: case ascertainment and control selection with matching criteria. Cross-sectional: sampling frame.

## 7. Variables
**Question:** Are all outcomes, exposures, predictors, confounders and effect modifiers clearly defined, with diagnostic criteria?
**Guidance:** Say how confounders were chosen, not only that they were adjusted for.

## 8. Data sources and measurement
**Question:** For each variable, are the sources of data and the methods of assessment given, with comparability across groups?
**Guidance:** Instrument, validation, blinding of assessors.

## 9. Bias
**Question:** What efforts were made to address potential sources of bias?
**Guidance:** Selection, information and confounding bias each need an answer.

## 10. Study size
**Question:** How was the study size arrived at?
**Guidance:** A power calculation, or an honest statement that the sample was what was available.

## 11. Quantitative variables
**Question:** How were quantitative variables handled in the analyses, and why were any groupings chosen?
**Guidance:** Categorising a continuous variable needs a justification.

## 12. Statistical methods
**Question:** Are the statistical methods described, including confounder control, subgroups, missing data, loss to follow-up or matching, and sensitivity analyses?
**Guidance:** Name the missing-data mechanism assumed and the method used.

## 13. Participants flow
**Question:** Are the numbers at each stage reported, with reasons for non-participation, and a flow diagram?
**Guidance:** Eligible, examined, confirmed eligible, included, followed up, analysed.

## 14. Descriptive data
**Question:** Are the characteristics of participants and information on exposures and confounders given, with missingness per variable?
**Guidance:** Cohort studies should also report follow-up time.

## 15. Outcome data
**Question:** Are outcome events or summary measures reported for the design used?
**Guidance:** Cohort: events over time. Case-control: exposure counts. Cross-sectional: outcome events or summary measures.

## 16. Main results
**Question:** Are unadjusted and confounder-adjusted estimates given with precision, and are the confounders stated?
**Guidance:** Say why each confounder was included. Report category boundaries when continuous variables were categorised.

## 17. Other analyses
**Question:** Are other analyses reported, including subgroups, interactions and sensitivity analyses?
**Guidance:** Label exploratory analyses.

## 18. Key results
**Question:** Are key results summarised with reference to the study objectives?
**Guidance:** Answer the question that was asked in item 3.

## 19. Limitations
**Question:** Are limitations discussed, including sources of potential bias or imprecision and their direction?
**Guidance:** Say which way each bias would push the estimate.

## 20. Interpretation
**Question:** Is a cautious overall interpretation given, considering objectives, limitations, multiplicity, and other evidence?
**Guidance:** Association is not causation; the wording should reflect that.

## 21. Generalisability
**Question:** Is the external validity of the results discussed?
**Guidance:** Name the population the estimate applies to.

## 22. Funding
**Question:** Is the source of funding and the role of the funders given, including for the original study if data were reused?
**Guidance:** State explicitly if there was no funding.
