# SRQR — Question Set (qualitative research, any method)

Source: Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research, https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/srqr/ (checked 2026-09-05).
Answer options: Yes / No / NA. Every answer needs a location or a reason.

This file paraphrases the 21 SRQR items. Use it for qualitative work that is
not interview or focus group based, or where COREQ fits badly: document
analysis, ethnography, case study, mixed qualitative designs.

## 1. Title
**Question:** Does the title identify the study as qualitative and name the method or approach?
**Guidance:** "A qualitative study", "an ethnographic study".

## 2. Abstract
**Question:** Does the abstract summarise the problem, approach, methods, results and implications?
**Guidance:** Follow the journal's structure.

## 3. Problem formulation
**Question:** Is the problem described, and its relevance to theory, practice or policy argued?
**Guidance:** Say why a qualitative approach is the right one for it.

## 4. Purpose or research question
**Question:** Are the purpose and specific objectives or questions stated?
**Guidance:** Qualitative questions ask how or why, not how many.

## 5. Qualitative approach and research paradigm
**Question:** Is the qualitative approach named and the guiding theory or paradigm identified, with a rationale?
**Guidance:** Grounded theory, phenomenology, narrative, case study, ethnography; constructivist, critical, pragmatist.

## 6. Researcher characteristics and reflexivity
**Question:** Are the researchers' characteristics and their relationship with participants described, with how these may affect the research?
**Guidance:** This is the reflexivity statement, and it must say what was done about the influence, not only that it existed.

## 7. Context
**Question:** Is the setting and the relevant context described?
**Guidance:** Enough for a reader to judge transferability.

## 8. Sampling strategy
**Question:** How were participants, documents or events selected, and why did sampling stop?
**Guidance:** Give the rationale, not just the label.

## 9. Ethical issues
**Question:** Are ethics approval, consent, and confidentiality measures documented?
**Guidance:** Name the approving body and the approval number.

## 10. Data collection methods
**Question:** Are the data collection methods described, including start and stop dates and any changes over time?
**Guidance:** Say how the procedure evolved and why.

## 11. Data collection instruments and technologies
**Question:** Are the instruments and technologies described, including changes during the study?
**Guidance:** Guides, observation protocols, recording equipment.

## 12. Units of study
**Question:** Are the number and relevant characteristics of participants, documents or events described?
**Guidance:** Include what was excluded.

## 13. Data processing
**Question:** Are the methods for processing data before and during analysis described?
**Guidance:** Transcription, de-identification, coding, anonymisation, data integrity checks.

## 14. Data analysis
**Question:** Is the process for arriving at the main findings described, including the analysts and their roles?
**Guidance:** Name the specific analytic technique, not just "themes emerged".

## 15. Techniques to enhance trustworthiness
**Question:** What techniques were used to enhance trustworthiness, and how do they fit the study?
**Guidance:** Triangulation, audit trail, negative case analysis, peer debriefing.

## 16. Synthesis and interpretation
**Question:** Are the main findings presented, with the development of theory or an interpretation of patterns?
**Guidance:** Move beyond describing the data.

## 17. Links to empirical data
**Question:** Is there evidence, such as quotations or field note excerpts, that substantiates the findings?
**Guidance:** Each analytic claim should have a data warrant.

## 18. Integration with prior work
**Question:** Are the findings integrated with prior work, and are the implications and the contribution stated?
**Guidance:** Say what changes now that this study exists.

## 19. Limitations
**Question:** Are the trustworthiness and limitations of the findings discussed?
**Guidance:** Address transferability explicitly.

## 20. Conflicts of interest
**Question:** Are potential sources of influence and conflicts of interest declared, with how they were managed?
**Guidance:** Including funder relationships.

## 21. Funding
**Question:** Are the sources of funding and their role in the study reported?
**Guidance:** Design, collection, analysis, reporting.
